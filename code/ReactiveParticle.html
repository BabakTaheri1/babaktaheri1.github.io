Power components

Equations and assumptions

The power triangle

This is a sinusoidal, single-phase model. Voltage and current magnitudes stay fixed, so S = VrmsIrms stays at 100 VA. Changing their relative phase changes P and Q.

P = S cos φ · Q = S sin φ · S² = P² + Q² · PF = P/S

Positive Q means current lags voltage; negative Q means it leads. The voltage and current chart uses normalized amplitudes to make the phase difference easy to see.

What the power chart shows

With v(t) = Vmax sin(ωt), the total instantaneous power is:

p(t) = P[1 − cos(2ωt)] − Q sin(2ωt)

The chart separates the nonnegative real-power component from the oscillating exchange component. Only the latter averages to zero. Q itself is a signed reactive-power quantity measured in var.

Why reactive power matters

Inductors and capacitors store energy and return it during each cycle. This exchange does no net work, but the associated current uses capacity in cables and transformers. At a fixed useful load and voltage, more current also means higher resistive losses.

The model leaves out harmonics, line losses, and changes in voltage. MIT’s AC circuit notes give the derivation.
