What the model includes, and what it leaves out

The dispatch calculation

Minimize energy offer cost and any emergency curtailment penalty subject to power balance, resource output bounds, and linear DC branch limits. Curtailment reduces load at its own bus. Demand response is a paid reduction in Metro load, represented as an equivalent injection.

min Σ cᵢPᵢ + 600 Σ sᵦ
qᵦ = Σᵢ at bus b Pᵢ + sᵦ − dᵦ
Σ qᵦ = 0 · |Hᵏq| ≤ Fᵏ
Pᵢ,min ≤ Pᵢ ≤ Pᵢ,max · 0 ≤ sᵦ ≤ dᵦ

P is resource dispatch, d is requested load, s is emergency load curtailment, and q is net bus injection. H maps injections to branch flows; k indexes the intact grid and, when enabled, the selected outage. One GW sustained for one hour is 1,000 MWh, so multiplying this objective by 1,000 gives the cost of the one-hour snapshot in dollars. Prices are reported in $/MWh.

In the real-time example, Pmin = max(online minimum, prior output − ramp × Δt) and Pmax = min(available capacity, prior output + ramp × Δt). Ramp rates are fictional GW/hour values and Δt = 1 hour. Real systems dispatch on a much shorter interval; PJM’s real-time energy market uses five minutes. The prior output is calculated independently from the previous hour’s intact-grid intraday example and stays fixed while you change demand or the outage check. Startup and shutdown trajectories are omitted.

The outage check removes the South–East line and recomputes all remaining flows. It checks that selected contingency, not every possible N-1 outage. Dispatchable headroom is unused output capability, excluding wind and solar; it is neither a reserve award nor a guarantee that extra power can be delivered through the network.

Nodal prices come from the dispatch problem’s linear-programming duals: the marginal offer cost of serving extra demand at each bus with commitment, availability, and ramp bounds held fixed. The energy/congestion split uses West as the reference bus; the total nodal price does not depend on that reference. At a change in the active constraints, a marginal price can be nonunique.

The illustrations

Day-ahead and intraday schedules are prescribed examples. There is no mixed-integer commitment solve, reserve co-optimization, or state-estimation calculation. Minimum up/down times and startup costs are not enforced. The state-estimation tab explains the measurement workflow; its dispatch example uses the same inputs as the intraday tab.

Losses, reactive power, voltage limits, storage energy balance, and market settlement rules are omitted. Storage is a discharge block, so playing hours does not charge or deplete a battery. Releasing fast-start blocks is a manual commitment change for the current snapshot, not an optimized start decision. The fleets, offers, ramp rates, and curtailment penalty are fictional. Actual processes and fast-start rules vary by market.
